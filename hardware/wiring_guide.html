How to actually solder this

If you've never done it, start here

Soldering is gluing with metal that conducts. You heat the pad, feed in solder, it melts and sticks to both the pad and the wire. That's all.

Tin both sides separately, then just melt them together. Far easier than doing it in one go.
What you seeWhat it means
Shiny, smooth, slightly concaveGood joint. Move on.
Dull, grey, lumpyMoved while cooling, or not hot enough. Re-heat until it flows, then hold still.
Ball sitting on top, not spreadThe pad wasn't hot — you melted solder onto a cold pad. Heat the pad, not the solder.
Blob bridging two padsShort circuit. Remove with braid or by dragging a clean hot tip across it.
Heat the pad, not the solder. That single sentence is 90% of learning to solder. Touch the iron to the pad, count two, then bring the solder in so the pad melts it. Melting solder on the tip and dabbing it on makes a joint that looks fine and falls off later.
Don't have an iron yet? The camera plugs in by ribbon — you can flash the board, run the server and read real labels today with zero soldering. See Vocalens_NoSolder_Day.pdf.

Front pod — six wires on the board

All on the XIAO
Five wires leave the pod as a bundle. The white touch wire stays inside.
  1. Cut the five long wires first. Measure your glasses from hinge to behind the ear, add 2cm. Too short can't be fixed.
  2. Tin all six pads: 3V3, GND, D8, D9, D10, D2.
  3. Solder the five bundle wires to the right-hand edge. Work top to bottom so your iron never crosses a finished joint.
  4. Solder the white wire to D2 on the left edge.
  5. Check for bridges. Look along the pad row at a low angle — a bridge is obvious side-on and invisible from above.
  6. Tug each wire gently. A joint that fails this on the bench will definitely fail on a face.
Keep the joints flat. Anything standing proud eats into the 10mm the pod has. If a blob is tall, re-heat it and let it settle rather than forcing the lid.

If it's too quiet, try 5V instead of 3V3. Most I2S amp modules run on 3–5V and get noticeably louder at 5V. Check your module is rated for it first, and note the XIAO's 5V pad only carries 5V while USB is plugged in — on battery it's dead. Start on 3V3; it's the safe one.
Many amp modules have extra pins — GAIN, SD, SHUTDOWN. Leave them unconnected. They have sensible defaults, and SD in particular usually means "shutdown" — grounding it silences the amp, which is a confusing hour to lose.

Test it in stages

Never debug two things at once
#TestPasses when
1Continuity, power OFF
Multimeter on beep mode		Each pad beeps to its far end, and no pad beeps to its neighbour. Neighbours beeping = a bridge.
2Power, nothing elsePlug in USB. Board enumerates, nothing gets hot. Anything warm — unplug immediately.
3Camera aloneServer receives a frame at /read_label. No audio yet.
4Audio, short wiresReply plays through the speaker. Prove it before committing to a 10cm run.
5Full-length wires, clipped onRepeat 3 and 4 with everything mounted.
6Touch padTapping triggers. Tune TOUCH_THRESHOLD while worn, not on the bench.
Test 1 costs two minutes and catches the mistake that kills boards. A solder bridge between 3V3 and GND is a dead short. Find it with a meter before you find it with smoke.
