Prepare the board

Read this page before you solder anything
20 min

The 7-pin headers: don't fit them

The XIAO ESP32S3 Sense is two boards: the XIAO itself, and the Sense expansion board underneath carrying the camera connector, PDM microphone and microSD slot, mated through a board-to-board connector. The supplied 2×7 headers insert from the top and their pins protrude downward — into exactly the space the expansion board occupies. That's why they won't go in. It isn't user error and nothing is broken.

Left: why the headers jam. Right: what to do instead.
Why skip the headers at all? You need six connections, not fourteen — and the pods are only 10 mm tall. That number is the entire reason they clear the wearer's head. A header strip plus its socket spends a chunk of that budget on pins you'll never use. Solder to the castellated pads instead.

If you do need to separate the two boards

  1. Unplug the camera ribbon first — release the dark flip-latch on the connector, then slide the ribbon out. Never pull it while latched.
  2. Pry evenly from the two short ends, a little at a time, alternating.
  3. Do not twist, and never lever against the camera connector — the most fragile thing on the board.
Before you power anything on: set PSRAM to OPI PSRAM in your build settings. If you skip this the camera silently fails to initialise and you will lose an hour to a bug that isn't in your code.

Tin the six pads now

Blob a little solder on each of the six pads before attaching any wires — it turns every later joint into a one-second touch rather than a three-handed wrestle inside a 10 mm cavity.

Wire the front pod

Camera, board, touch pad
30 min
Parts for this step
  • ×1XIAO ESP32S3 Sense, camera attached, pads tinned
  • ×5Jumper wires, cut to temple length + 2cm slack — red, black, yellow, green, blue
  • ×1Jumper wire, white, ~4cm
  • ×1Metal disc — a coin, screw head, or scrap of foil
  • ×1frontboard_base (TPU) and frontboard_lid (PETG)
Front pod, viewed from above with the lid off. The camera sits beside the board, not on top of it.
  1. Cut the five temple wires to length first. Measure along your actual glasses from the hinge to behind the ear, then add 2cm. Too short is unrecoverable; too long just tucks away.
  2. Solder red to 3V3, black to GND, yellow to D7, green to D8, blue to D9. Keep the joints flat — anything standing proud eats your lid clearance.
  3. Solder the white wire to D3. This one stays inside the pod.
  4. Seat the camera in the front of the cavity, lens aligned with the hole in the base. A dab of hot glue holds it.
  5. Seat the XIAO beside it. Do not stack the camera on the board — side by side is what keeps the pod at 10mm and off the wearer's head.
  6. Route the five temple wires out through the rear-face slot, and the USB cable out the side slot.
Test before you close the lid. Power the board over USB and confirm the camera initialises. Re-opening a hot-glued pod is miserable; catching a dry joint now costs you thirty seconds.

Rear pod from above, lid off. Amp and speaker sit side by side along the arm.
Wire inFrom (front pod)To (amp)

  1. Feed the five temple wires in through the front-face slot of the rear pod.
  2. Connect each to the amplifier per the table above. Check the silkscreen labels — some boards print SCK for BCLK and WS for LRC. They're the same signals.
  3. Connect the speaker to the amp's two output terminals. Polarity doesn't matter with a single speaker.
  4. Seat the amp and speaker side by side, speaker face pointing outward, under the grille holes in the lid.
  5. Leave the lid off for now — Step 5 tests audio before you close it.
Why jumper wires and not a ribbon cable? I2S is a slow serial protocol. A 10cm run is electrically fine, and it means this whole build costs nothing beyond what the event handed every team. Say that in the pitch before a judge notices the exposed wires — it reads as a deliberate constraint, not an oversight.

Touch pad, power, and clip-on

Closing it up
20 min

The touch pad

There is no touch sensor component. The ESP32's capacitive touch peripheral reads a bare GPIO pin directly — the wire and any conductive surface are the whole circuit.

  1. Glue a coin, screw head, or scrap of foil into the recess on the outside of frontboard_lid.
  2. Solder the white wire from D3 to it. If it's a coin, scuff the surface first so solder takes.
  3. Press the lid on. The pad should be reachable with a fingertip when the glasses are worn.
Tune TOUCH_THRESHOLD once assembled, not before. The reading changes with the pad's size, the glue, and whether the wearer's head is nearby. Calibrating on the bench and skipping this step is the classic source of a device that false-triggers all through the demo.

Power

OptionHowUse it when
USB-CCable out the front pod's side slot to a laptop or power bankDemo night. Simplest and most reliable — use this.

Clipping onto the glasses

  1. Clip the front pod on near the hinge, camera facing forward along the wearer's line of sight.
  2. Clip the rear pod on behind the ear.
  3. Run the five wires along the top edge of the temple arm, with a dot of removable adhesive so they don't snag.
  4. Put the glasses on. Check nothing presses into the head and the arm doesn't splay.
Both pods sit beside the arm, not on top of it. That's why they're thin vertically (10mm) and long outward (29–38mm). A temple arm rests against the skull — there's no room above or below it, but plenty straight out. If a pod is touching the wearer's head, something is oriented wrong; check it before gluing.

Bring it up — in this order

Each stage is testable alone
Allow an evening
Do not try to make everything work at once. Every stage you skip here is a bug you'll be hunting at 2am with four variables in play instead of one.
#StageWhat proves it works
1Camera only, on the bench, pods not yet connected to each otherFlash, trigger the touch pad, confirm the server receives a frame at /read_label. No audio, no mic.
2Audio out, still on the bench, with short wires between the podsThe server's spoken reply plays through the speaker.
3Full temple-length wires, both pods clipped onRe-test 1 and 2. A longer run occasionally needs a pull-up tweak — I2S at this length usually doesn't.
4Microphone inRecord a spoken question, confirm the server transcribes it.
5Wake word — lastHighest-risk item in the project. The touch pad already gives you a complete working demo without it.
Stage 2 uses short wires deliberately. Prove the audio chain works over 5cm before you commit to a 10cm run threaded along a temple arm. If it breaks after Stage 3, you know it's the cable and not the amp.

SymptomMost likely cause

Static, buzzing, or noise onlyBCLK and LRC swapped. Yellow → BCLK, green → LRC.

Print a spare set of pods before demo night. TPU bases are quick, and a cracked clip an hour before you present is the difference between a demo and an apology.
