Prep — before the iron touches anything

Cut 5 long wires — red, black, yellow, green, blue.
Hinge → behind the ear on YOUR glasses, plus 2cm. Too short can't be fixed.
Cut 1 white wire, ~5cm
Strip 3mm off both ends of all six. Twist each end tight.
Iron to ~350°C. Tip shiny — if dull or black, wipe it and melt a little solder onto it.
Check your amp module. Header pins already fitted? Then its 5 connections are push-on, no iron.

The pad map — wire by PAD LABEL, not GPIO number

Five wires in a row on the right edge, one on the left. Nothing else is touched.

The move — tin, tin, join

Do it in three parts, never in one go
Tinning both sides first turns each joint into a one-second touch instead of a three-handed wrestle.
Heat the PAD, not the solder.
That one sentence is most of learning to solder. Iron on the pad, count two, then bring the solder in so the pad melts it. Melting solder on the tip and dabbing it on makes a joint that looks fine and falls off later.

Reading your own joints

What you seeWhat to do
Shiny, smooth, slightly dishedGood. Move on.
Dull, grey, lumpyYou moved it while cooling. Re-heat till it flows, hold still. Don't add solder.
Ball sat on top, not spread outPad was cold. Re-heat the pad properly and it'll wet.
Blob bridging two padsFix now. Drag a clean hot tip across it, or use braid.

Joints 1–6 — the XIAO

Tin all six pads first, then all six wires, then join
Work top to bottom down the right edge. That way the iron never has to reach across a joint you've already made.
✓#PADWIREOther end goes to
1GNDBlackAmp GND
23V3RedAmp VIN
3D10BlueAmp DIN
4D9GreenAmp LRC / WS
5D8YellowAmp BCLK / SCK
6D2 (left edge)WhiteTouch disc — joint 9
Tug every wire gently before you move on. A joint that pulls off on the bench will definitely pull off on someone's face.
Keep the blobs flat. The pod only has 10mm of height. If a joint stands proud, re-heat and let it settle rather than forcing the lid down on it.
Found a bridge? Wipe the tip clean, then drag it firmly across the bridge away from the pads. The solder follows the hot tip off the board. Re-check from the side. Don't add flux-less solder trying to "push" it apart — that makes it bigger.

Joints 7–9, and the amp end

Nearly done
✓#What
7Short wire → speaker terminal one
8Short wire → speaker terminal two
9White wire's free end → the scuffed metal disc
—Speaker's two free ends → amp OUT+ and OUT−
—The five bundle wires → amp VIN GND BCLK LRC DIN
Leave GAIN, SD and SHUTDOWN unconnected. They have sensible defaults. SD usually means shutdown — grounding it silences the amp completely, and that is a very confusing hour to lose.
Amp label variants — all the same signals:
BCLK = SCK = CLK · LRC = WS = LRCLK · DIN = SD (data) = DATA

Check before you plug in

Two minutes. Do not skip it.
A bridge between 3V3 and GND is a dead short.
It can kill the board the instant you plug in. Find it with a meter, not with smoke.

Multimeter on beep mode — still unplugged

✓Touch these twoExpected
3V3 and GNDMUST NOT BEEP
D8 and D9must not beep
D9 and D10must not beep
D10 and 3V3must not beep
Each pad → its own far wire endmust beep
Any beep in the first four rows = a solder bridge. Find it, drag a clean hot tip across it, re-test. Don't plug in until all four are silent.

Then, and only then

  1. Plug in USB.
  2. Touch the board and amp within 5 seconds. More than mildly warm — unplug immediately, re-check for bridges.
  3. Board shows up on your laptop → flash it.
  4. Camera test first, audio second. Never both at once.
